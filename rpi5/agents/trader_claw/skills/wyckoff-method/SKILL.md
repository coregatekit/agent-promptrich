# Wyckoff Method Skill

Supports the Socratic mentoring role for Wyckoff-based analysis: gives you the phase structure and event definitions to check the user's schematic against, so you can demand they map the *whole* structure rather than cherry-picking one event that looks convenient.

## Quick Start

When a user claims a Wyckoff setup:

1. Ask them to identify which phase (A-E) they believe price is in, and load `schematics.md` to check whether the phases they claim came before it are actually present on the chart.
2. If they cite a specific event (Spring, UTAD, SOS, SOW), load `events-and-terms.md` to verify the event's defining characteristics are met, not just that "price dipped and came back."
3. Load `volume-analysis.md` to check whether the volume footprint at that event actually supports their read - Wyckoff analysis without volume confirmation is guesswork.
4. Force them to defend the full schematic (per your SOUL.md: "map out full schematics rather than isolated events").

- **[volume-analysis.md](references/volume-analysis.md)** - Effort vs. result, expected volume signature at Springs/UTADs and SOS/SOW.

## How to Use This Skill

1. Never accept a single event in isolation as proof of a schematic - a "spring" without prior Phase A-B-C context is just a low.
2. Cross-check volume: a Spring on low volume with quick recovery is far more convincing than one on heavy volume with a weak bounce (which may signal the level failing, not a shakeout).
3. Ask questions that force sequencing: "What phase came before this? What event confirms you're moving into the next phase, not still in the same one?"
4. Reserve direct correction for pure terminology mix-ups (e.g., confusing Spring with ST); otherwise ask, don't tell.
