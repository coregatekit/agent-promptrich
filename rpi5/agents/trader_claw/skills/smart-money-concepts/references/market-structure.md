# Market Structure: MSS, BOS, CHOCH, and Multi-Timeframe Alignment

## Core Structure Concepts

- **Higher High / Higher Low (HH/HL):** bullish structure. Price makes a swing high above the prior swing high, and pulls back to a low above the prior swing low.
- **Lower Low / Lower High (LL/LH):** bearish structure.
- **Swing point:** a local high or low confirmed once price closes back through it (not just a wick).

## Break of Structure (BOS)

- Occurs when price breaks the most recent swing point **in the direction of the prevailing trend**.
- A bullish BOS = a new higher high in an uptrend. It confirms trend continuation, not a reversal.
- Validity check: the break should close beyond the swing point (a wick-only poke is weak evidence).

## Change of Character (CHOCH)

- Occurs when price breaks a swing point **against** the prevailing trend for the first time - the first sign the trend may be reversing.
- Example: in a downtrend (LL/LH), price breaks above the most recent lower high → CHOCH bullish.
- CHOCH is a warning/alert, not confirmation. It usually needs a follow-through BOS in the new direction to be considered a full structural shift.

## Market Structure Shift (MSS)

- Umbrella term some traders use interchangeably with CHOCH, but stricter usage reserves MSS for a *confirmed* shift: CHOCH followed by structure that behaves consistently with the new trend (e.g., a subsequent higher low holds after a bullish CHOCH).
- Push the user to specify: are they calling a shift on the CHOCH alone, or do they have confirmation?

## Common Mistakes to Probe For

- Calling a BOS a CHOCH (or vice versa) without checking what the *prevailing* trend actually was beforehand.
- Using a wick to justify a break instead of a close.
- Declaring a shift off a single timeframe without checking if higher timeframe structure still disagrees.

## Multi-Timeframe (HTF → LTF) Alignment

- The standard workflow: establish HTF (e.g., 4H/Daily) bias first, then only take LTF (e.g., 15m/5m) setups that align with it.
- A CHOCH on LTF against an intact HTF trend is often just a retracement/liquidity grab, not a real reversal - this is exactly where users get trapped.
- Questions to demand: "What is HTF structure doing right now?" "Does your LTF entry trade with or against it?" "If against it, what specific evidence justifies treating this as more than a pullback?"
