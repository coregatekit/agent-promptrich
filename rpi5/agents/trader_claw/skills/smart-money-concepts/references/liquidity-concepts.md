# Liquidity: BSL/SSL, Equal Highs/Lows, Inducement, Sweeps

## Buy-Side / Sell-Side Liquidity (BSL / SSL)

- **BSL:** resting buy orders/stops sitting above prior highs (retail stop-losses on shorts, breakout buy orders).
- **SSL:** resting sell orders/stops sitting below prior lows.
- Price is drawn toward these pools because large orders need counter-liquidity to fill against.

## Equal Highs / Equal Lows (EQH / EQL)

- Multiple swing highs (or lows) at roughly the same price level = an obvious, "advertised" liquidity pool - the more times a level is tested without breaking, the more stops accumulate there.
- EQH/EQL are prime sweep targets, not necessarily strong resistance/support in the classical sense.

## Inducement

- A minor structure point placed intentionally (from a smart-money-concepts read) to trap early entries before the real move.
- Often a small swing high/low just before a larger liquidity pool, designed to get retail traders positioned the wrong way so their stops become the fuel for the sweep.
- Ask the user: "Is the level you're using an inducement point or the actual liquidity target? How do you tell the difference here?"

## Liquidity Sweep vs. Genuine Breakout

| Signal | Liquidity Sweep | Genuine Breakout |
|---|---|---|
| Move beyond level | Quick wick/spike through, fast rejection | Sustained close(s) beyond level |
| Volume/momentum | Often a blow-off spike then reversal | Steady follow-through |
| What follows | Sharp reversal back through the level | Continuation, level acts as new support/resistance (flip) |

## Questions to Probe

- "Whose liquidity is being targeted here - yours, or the other side's?"
- "Did price just sweep SSL/BSL and reverse, or did it actually break structure with a close?"
- "If this is a sweep, where's your evidence of a reversal signal (CHOCH, rejection candle, order block) immediately after - or are you just assuming it because the level was touched?"
