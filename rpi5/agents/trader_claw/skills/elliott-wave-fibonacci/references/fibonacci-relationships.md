# Fibonacci Relationships in Elliott Wave

## Standard Retracement Ratios

- Common levels: 23.6%, 38.2%, 50%, 61.8%, 78.6%.
- **Wave 2** commonly retraces 50%-61.8% of wave 1 (can go deeper, but never past 100%).
- **Wave 4** commonly retraces 23.6%-38.2% of wave 3 (typically shallower than wave 2, per the alternation guideline).
- **Wave B** in a zigzag commonly retraces 38.2%-61.8% of wave A.

## Standard Extension Ratios

- **Wave 3** commonly extends to 161.8% of wave 1 (measured from the end of wave 2).
- **Wave 5** commonly relates to wave 1 by equality, or to the total of waves 1-3 by 61.8%, when wave 3 is extended.
- **Wave C** commonly equals wave A, or extends to 161.8% of wave A.

## Confluence: Why a Single Ratio Isn't Enough

- A price target is only meaningful when **multiple independent Fibonacci relationships cluster near the same price** - e.g., wave 3 = 161.8% of wave 1 *and* wave 5 = 61.8% of waves 1-3 land within a tight range of each other.
- A target based on one ratio alone (e.g., "61.8% retracement, so it must bounce here") is a coin flip - markets tap and blow through single Fib levels constantly.
- Time-based Fibonacci (Fibonacci time sequences/zones) is a secondary, weaker confirmation tool - never accept it as the primary justification for a target.

## Probing Questions

- "What other ratio or wave relationship lines up near this level besides the one you quoted?"
- "Is this retracement level consistent with alternation - if wave 2 was deep, do you expect wave 4 to be shallow, and does your number reflect that?"
- "If price blows through this Fib level without reacting, what does that tell you about your wave count itself?"
- "You're using a single 61.8% level as your entire thesis - where's the confluence?"
