# Corrective Patterns: Zigzags, Flats, Triangles, Combinations

## Zigzag (5-3-5, labeled A-B-C)

- Sharp, deep correction. Wave A subdivides in 5, wave B in 3 (and is typically short, retracing less than ~61.8% of wave A), wave C subdivides in 5 and often moves beyond the end of wave A.
- **Double/Triple zigzag:** two or three zigzags linked by an intervening wave labeled X (e.g., W-X-Y, or W-X-Y-X-Z).

## Flat (3-3-5, labeled A-B-C)

- **Regular flat:** wave B retraces close to 100% of wave A; wave C is roughly equal to wave A.
- **Expanded flat:** wave B moves *beyond* the start of wave A (a new extreme), and wave C extends well beyond the end of wave A - the most common flat variant in modern markets.
- **Running flat:** wave B exceeds wave A's start, but wave C fails to reach the end of wave A - signals strong underlying trend pressure (rare, treat claims skeptically).

## Triangle (labeled A-B-C-D-E, each leg subdividing in 3)

- **Contracting:** converging trendlines, each wave smaller than the last - typically appears in wave 4, or wave B/X position, signaling continuation after completion.
- **Expanding (rare):** diverging trendlines, each wave larger than the last.
- Triangles typically precede the *final* move of the larger pattern they're in - if a user claims a triangle then expects another triangle immediately after, treat that as a red flag.

## Combinations

- **Double/triple three:** two or three corrective patterns (flat, zigzag, or triangle) linked by X waves, labeled W-X-Y(-X-Z).
- Used when a single simple correction doesn't adequately explain a long, complex sideways period.

## Probing Questions

- "Does your wave B retrace close to 100% of wave A, or go beyond it? That distinguishes a regular flat from an expanded flat - which are you claiming?"
- "If this is a zigzag, is wave C subdividing into 5 waves? Show me the five legs."
- "If you're calling this a triangle, are the trendlines actually converging, and does each wave get smaller than the last?"
- "Why a single corrective pattern instead of a combination - does one simple A-B-C actually explain all the price action you're pointing at?"
